1080×1920 · 9:16 · 52 sec

On screen
Pomfret or surmai? Most people pick wrong for their recipe.
Visual
Both fish side by side on dark surface. Camera pulls back slowly from close-up to show both.
3–45 sec · PAYOFF
Spoken
Pomfret — also known as paplet — is flat, mild, and delicate. The flesh flakes easily. It is perfect for light preparations: steamed, fried whole, or in a coconut-based curry where you want the fish flavour to be subtle. The texture is soft and the taste is gentle. Pomfret is forgiving — even slightly old pomfret can taste reasonable because its flavour is so mild to begin with. Surmai — king mackerel — is the opposite. Firm, oily, bold. Strong flavour. It holds up to heavy spicing. Recheado masala, strong red curry, grilling. Surmai ages badly — the oiliness turns fast. So surmai is the fish where freshness matters most. If you are buying surmai from a delivery app during the fishing ban — it is cold storage. The oils have already started to turn. Buy surmai only when you know the source and the date. Right now, during the ban — stick to prawns, bhetki, rohu. When the ban lifts and wild surmai is back — that is when to cook it.
