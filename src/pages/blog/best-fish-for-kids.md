# Which Fish Is Best for Kids?

**For children, start with mild fish that has few bones: pomfret, boneless basa or bhetki fillets, and rawas or surmai steaks.** These are easy to eat and gentle in flavour. Save bony fish like bangda, rohu and katla for later.

*This guide is about taste, texture and bones, not medical advice. Ask your doctor about allergies or when to introduce fish.*

---

## Easiest fish to start with

| Fish | Why it suits children | Ask the seller for |
|---|---|---|
| [Pomfret](/fish/pomfret) | Soft and mild; one central spine, few small bones | Cleaned whole fish, or curry pieces |
| [Basa](/fish/basa) | Very mild; sold as boneless fillets | Boneless fillets or fingers |
| [Bhetki](/fish/bhetki) | Mild, firm white fillets | Boneless fillets |
| [Rawas](/fish/rawas) | Mild, firm, flaky white flesh | Steaks (one central bone) |
| [Surmai](/fish/surmai) | Meaty; steaks with one central bone | Steaks |

## Fish to wait on

- [Bangda (mackerel)](/fish/bangda): strong flavour and fine bones.
- [Rohu](/fish/rohu) and [katla](/fish/katla): freshwater carp with many fine bones.
- [Bombil](/fish/bombil): very soft, but has a central bone. Fine once children are used to fish.

---

## Tips for serving fish to children

1. **Ask for boneless cuts or steaks.** Sellers on Relifish let you choose whole, cleaned or cut.
2. **Check for bones yourself.** Flake the cooked fish with your fingers before serving.
3. **Keep the masala mild.** Turmeric, salt and a little lemon, then shallow-fry or steam.
4. **Cook fish the same day.** Fresh fish tastes milder; older fish smells and tastes stronger.

## How to check fish is fresh

1. Clear eyes and bright red gills
2. Firm flesh that springs back
3. A clean sea smell, never sour or of ammonia
