# The Sunday Fish Ritual: Why Mumbai Families Refuse to Let This Tradition Die

Ask anyone who grew up in a Mumbai coastal home what Sunday smells like.

They won't say chai. They won't say newspaper. They'll say: *coconut oil hitting a hot pan. Fish marinating in turmeric. The pressure cooker hissing with rice.*

In Koli homes, in Mangalorean apartments along Ghodbunder Road, in Bengali families in Majiwada, in Maharashtrian households across Hiranandani Estate — Sunday lunch is fish. It has always been fish. And it will always be fish.

This is that story.

---

## Why Sunday Fish Is Not About Fish

The thing about the Sunday fish ritual is that it was never really about the fish.

It's about the grandmother who insists on cleaning it herself even though her knees hurt. It's about the husband who wakes up at 7 AM to go to the market because that's what his father did. It's about the children who know that when the kitchen smells like that, the family is together.

Priya Kamath, a working mother in Hiranandani Estate, told us something that stuck: *"The one thing my 9-year-old asks for every Sunday is 'Amma's fish curry.' Not pizza. Not pasta. Fish curry. That's home for him."*

This is what fresh fish does in a Mumbai household. It doesn't just feed bodies. It anchors weeks. It marks time. It says: this family is together, right now, this Sunday.

## What's Actually in That Curry

The science behind why fish curry makes people feel *good* — genuinely, biologically good — is worth understanding.

A traditional pomfret or surmai curry cooked in coconut base delivers:

- **Omega-3 DHA** — directly supports mood regulation and reduces family stress hormones (yes, really)
- **High-quality complete protein** — all 9 essential amino acids, more bioavailable than chicken
- **Vitamin D** — one of the most deficient nutrients in urban Indian families who spend most time indoors
- **Selenium and Iodine** — critical for thyroid function, affecting energy levels and mental clarity

The reason Sunday fish lunch feels different from other meals isn't just nostalgia. Your body is actually *responding* to the nutrients.

## The Threat to the Ritual Nobody Talks About

Here's what nobody says out loud but every Mumbai family knows: **the Sunday fish ritual is getting harder to maintain.**

The local fish seller closes by 9 AM. If you missed him, you're at the mercy of a supermarket selling two-day-old pomfret wrapped in plastic that smells of refrigerant. Or a delivery app showing "fresh" fish that left a Chennai warehouse on Thursday.

Families are slowly, quietly substituting. Chicken replaces fish on Sundays when the stall was sold out. Restaurant delivery replaces the home-cooked ritual when the market run felt too exhausting. The family still sits together — but something is missing. The grandmother knows. She doesn't say anything.

This is the small erosion that worries us most.
