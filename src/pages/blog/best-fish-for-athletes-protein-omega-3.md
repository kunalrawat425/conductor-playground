# Best Fish for Athletes and Gym-Goers

**If you run, lift, swim or play cricket or football, fish gives you lean protein, and some fish also give you the omega-3 fats EPA and DHA.** Of all the fish below, [surmai](/fish/surmai) has the most protein (22.28 g per 100 g), and it has the most EPA + DHA after salmon. [Bangda](/fish/bangda) is close on protein with less fat. [Prawns](/fish/prawns), [bhetki](/fish/bhetki) and [bombil](/fish/bombil) are the lightest on calories.

Every number in this post comes from a published food table, listed under [Sources](#sources) at the bottom. All values are **per 100 g raw edible portion** (the flesh you eat, without head, bones or shell), not per 100 g of whole fish.

*This is general food information, not medical or dietary advice. See [who should check with a doctor](#who-should-check-with-a-doctor-first).*

**How to read this table**

- Protein, fat and EPA + DHA for every fish except rawas are from IFCT 2017 (NIN Hyderabad). IFCT gives energy in kilojoules; we converted to kcal with IFCT's own factor (1 kcal = 4.18 kJ) and rounded to whole numbers. IFCT reports EPA and DHA separately in mg; we added them and converted to grams.
- Rawas is not in IFCT 2017. Its values are from the Philippine Food Composition Table (DOST-FNRI) for the same species, *Eleutheronema tetradactylum*. That table does not report EPA or DHA, so that cell is blank.
- Ghol (*Protonibea diacanthus*) is not in IFCT 2017 or USDA FoodData Central, and we found no published table with its flesh values, so the row is left blank rather than guessed.
- IFCT values are averages of samples collected across India. Any one fish can differ with size, season and where it was caught. Prawn values vary by prawn species; the row above is tiger prawns.

## Fish vs chicken, eggs, paneer and dal

Per 100 g, raw unless stated.

| Food | Energy (kcal) | Protein (g) | Fat (g) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Surmai | 136 | 22.28 | 5.18 | IFCT 2017 |
| Bangda | 101 | 21.51 | 1.53 | IFCT 2017 |
| Pomfret (white) | 123 | 19.02 | 5.12 | IFCT 2017 |
| Chicken breast, skinless, raw | 120 | 22.5 | 2.62 | USDA FoodData Central |
| Egg, whole, raw | 143 | 12.56 | 9.51 | USDA FoodData Central |
| Paneer | 306 | 18.86 | 24.78 | IFCT 2017 |
| Toor dal (red gram dal), dry, uncooked | 331 | 21.70 | 1.56 | IFCT 2017 |
| Lentils, cooked (boiled, no salt) | 116 | 9.02 | 0.38 | USDA FoodData Central |

What this shows:

- **Surmai and bangda match chicken breast on protein.** USDA's skinless chicken breast is 22.5 g; surmai is 22.28 g and bangda 21.51 g.
- **Chicken breast is also very lean.** Fish's clear difference is omega-3: USDA lists raw chicken breast at 0.007 g EPA + DHA per 100 g, against 0.33 g for bangda and 1.05 g for surmai. A whole raw egg has 0.058 g.
- **Chicken fat varies.** IFCT 2017's Indian samples of skinless chicken breast came out at 21.81 g protein and 9.00 g fat per 100 g, much higher fat than the USDA figure. Fat depends on the bird and how it is trimmed.
- **Paneer has protein, with a lot of fat.** 18.86 g protein comes with 24.78 g fat.
- **Dal numbers depend on whether it is dry or cooked.** 100 g of dry toor dal has 21.70 g protein, but dal absorbs water when cooked. USDA's cooked lentils give 9.02 g per 100 g.

## Fish meals before and after training

These are everyday Indian ways to eat fish around training. How long before a session you can eat comfortably is personal; the International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) says protein before or after resistance training both help, and that timing is "likely a matter of individual tolerance".

**Before training (light and easy to digest)**

- Rice with a thin [bombil](/fish/bombil) or [rawas](/fish/rawas) curry, light on oil
- Steamed or grilled [bhetki](/fish/bhetki) or [pomfret](/fish/pomfret) with rice or a chapati
- [Prawn](/fish/prawns) pulao with less oil

**After training (protein plus carbohydrate)**

- [Surmai](/fish/surmai) or [bangda](/fish/bangda) tawa fry (little oil) with rice or bhakri and a salad
- Fish curry with [rohu](/fish/rohu) or [katla](/fish/katla) and rice
- Grilled [tarli](/fish/sardine) or [salmon](/fish/salmon) with chapati and vegetables
- Egg bhurji with a side of prawns, if you want two protein sources in one meal

## How much fish per meal

The ISSN position stand on protein and exercise suggests:

- **1.4 to 2.0 g of protein per kg of body weight per day** is enough for most people who exercise.
- **About 0.25 g per kg per meal, or 20 to 40 g of protein per meal.**
- **Spread these doses evenly, every 3 to 4 hours,** through the day.

A worked example using the table above: 150 g of raw edible bangda gives about 32 g of protein (1.5 × 21.51 g). Remember this is the weight of the flesh. A whole fish with head and bones weighs more than the part you eat, so ask the seller for cleaned weight if you are tracking closely.

## How to cook fish and keep it lean

The table above is for raw fish. Cooking method decides how much fat you add.

1. **Grill, steam or bake** with a masala rub (turmeric, chilli, lemon, ginger-garlic).
2. **Tawa fry with a teaspoon of oil** instead of deep frying. Skip the thick rava or besan coating if you want it lighter.
3. **Curry with less oil and less coconut.** A Konkani-style curry with kokum or a tomato-onion base works well.
4. **Do not overcook.** Fish is done when the flesh turns opaque and flakes easily; overcooking only dries it out.
5. **Buy it cleaned and cut.** Sellers on Relifish let you choose whole, cleaned or cut, so steaks or fillets are ready for the tawa.

## Mercury: which fish to choose

The US FDA groups fish by mercury level. Its **"Best Choices"** (lowest mercury) list includes sardine, Atlantic mackerel, Pacific chub mackerel, salmon, shrimp, crab, squid, mullet and sole. Its **"Choices to Avoid"** list includes king mackerel, shark, swordfish, marlin and bigeye tuna.

Two things to keep in mind:

- The FDA list is written for fish sold in the US. Indian mackerel (bangda) and surmai (*Scomberomorus commerson*) are not named on it, so we do not put them in either group.
- If mercury worries you, small fish on the "Best Choices" list, like sardine (tarli), are the simplest pick. The FDA puts shark on its avoid list, which also covers mushi.

## Who should check with a doctor first

Talk to your doctor or a registered dietitian before changing how much fish or protein you eat if you:

- Are pregnant, planning a pregnancy or breastfeeding. The FDA's advice for this group is 2 to 3 servings a week from its "Best Choices" list, with 1 serving being 4 ounces.
- Are feeding children. The FDA has separate serving advice for children aged 1 to 11.
- Have a seafood or shellfish allergy.
- Have a kidney condition or any condition where your doctor has set a protein limit.
- Are on a prescribed diet or medication that your doctor has linked to food.

## FAQ

**Which fish has the most protein?**
Among the fish in the table, surmai has the most: 22.28 g per 100 g raw edible portion (IFCT 2017). Bangda is next at 21.51 g.

**Which fish has the most omega-3?**
Salmon has the most EPA + DHA in the table (1.60 g per 100 g). Among Indian sea fish, surmai is highest (1.05 g), followed by halwa (0.81 g), tarli (0.61 g) and pomfret (0.59 g).

**Is fish better than chicken for protein?**
On protein alone they are close. Skinless chicken breast is 22.5 g per 100 g (USDA); surmai is 22.28 g and bangda 21.51 g (IFCT). Fish has much more EPA + DHA than chicken breast.

**Which fish is lowest in calories?**
Prawns (65 kcal), bhetki (68 kcal) and bombil (69 kcal) per 100 g raw edible portion are the lowest in the table.

**Is freshwater fish like rohu good for protein?**
Yes. Rohu has 19.71 g and katla 17.94 g protein per 100 g, both with under 2.5 g fat (IFCT 2017).

**Do the numbers change after cooking?**
Yes. All values here are for raw fish. Fish loses water as it cooks and frying adds oil, so cooked numbers differ. We have not listed cooked values because our sources give raw values for these fish.
